Let me Start off with some of the things that has been going on for me, just kind of ranting about what my life is right now. Marching band camp is going on and its deadly schedule is absolutely killing me and giving me no free time to even do anything. Things I had to do over the summer: relax, college applications, sat prep, read, chem homework. They are all just not getting accomplished. Which was an stupid move from me to not do them earlier, especially given that I have gone through marching band a couple of times before. 

Now Lets talk about The Notes From Underground, which I finished a couple of days ago, but didn't get time to write about it until now. And I also do want to clarify that I have likely missed many things from the book, because I was only focused on the one thing that was interesting for me. 

In the first section where the author just goes through a series of making philosophical claims without supporting them, I read through them and thought "Oh, this all makes sense", and one that stood out the most for me was that everyone does things because they see justice in it and deep inside they feel that it is the most rational choice. It made a lot of sense, because if you are the one making the decision, wouldn't you not do it if you felt like it was irrational to do such a thing? Yeah that was my thinking, but the author decides to tell me that isn't true at the end of the novel. 

When the author is getting mad at Liza for witnessing him in unfavorable clothes, he knows that he is doing something wrong, but his action does otherwise. Even after doing it, he deeply regrets doing so. He knew he had to stop, but he couldn't stop. This tells me that what you want to do may be different from what your brain wants to do, and sometimes what your brain wants to do cannot be controlled. 

Well, this explains a lot. Why would I have not studied all these time while my mind wanted to study and knew that I should have been studying? Why am I still chasing acceptance of others even when I have recognized that it didn't have much value? I clearly have decided that it was the wrong thing to do, but I just cannot control it. It's my body, but I cannot control it. Weird enough, that's where my conclusion will be, because I cannot figure out any solution. The book also ended in a way similar to this, so that's where I am right now.
